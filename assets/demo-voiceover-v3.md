# TerraTunes 30 s demo: voiceover v3

Changes from v2: line 4 now names more than one dataset, and the last line credits the team. **Only lines 4 and 7 are new.** Lines 1–3, 5 and 6 match the v2 take word for word, so you can re-record just the two new lines and splice them in.

## Read-through (paste into the voice tool)

```
Every day, NASA paints the whole planet.

But millions of people will never see those paintings.

So Terra Tunes plays them.

Hear where the oceans run hot, where storms pour down, and so much more.

Every note comes from a real NASA measurement, and the Data Deck shows you which one.

Got a NASA picture of your own? Scan it, and it sings too.

Terra Tunes, by team Bit and Beat. Now everyone can hear the Earth.
```

About 75 words, roughly 23 s of speech. Leave a short pause between lines; the film has gaps of 0.3–0.9 s between them.

## Delivery for the two new lines

- **Line 4:** three beats, getting lighter. Say "oceans run hot" with warmth, "storms pour down" with a little weight, and "so much more" with a smile. It has to fit in about 4.2 s, so keep it moving.
- **Line 7:** "Terra Tunes" as two words, then a light, proud "by team Bit and Beat". Take a short breath before "Now everyone can hear the Earth" and let it land. Finish by 29.3 s so there's a moment of quiet before the cut.

## Why the claims hold

- **Line 4:** each record's pitch and loudness follow its own measured values (docs/SONIFICATION_DESIGN.md). Warm water and heavy rain both stand out in the sound, and the film shows each map being played as the line names it.
- **"So much more":** there are nine records in all, and the film shows the other seven as the line ends.
